--- layout: post title: "Summer fun: a 50 mile bike-ride through the East Bay hills" date: 2026-07-19 tags: [random] read_time: "5 min read" description: "I nearly died" mathjax: true --- At the start of summer, I got a really weird obsession with the idea of biking up to Berkeley from the East Bay, a distance of almost 50 miles. In my childhood, I would spend hours biking around the local neighborhood and park every day. At college, apart from sparse use of a friend's bike, I almost never biked. Despite this 7-year hiatus, I was undeterred.

The route

The easiest way to Berkeley is nearer the coast of the Bay, where it is mostly flat, with a small climb towards the end. But in my mind, it wasn't very scenic; I wanted a real biking experience with hills and trees and a good view. So instead, the route breaks down into four legs:
  • Warmup: ~10 flat miles east, finishing along Highway 84.
  • Palomares Road: a 5 mile, ~1300 ft climb, then 10 mostly-downhill miles to 580.
  • Redwood Rd: the brutal segment, with 2500 ft of gain spread across two climbs. First to ~1000 ft over 3 miles near Lake Chabot, then a descent into Redwood Regional Park, then a second climb over 5 miles to 1460 ft, cresting near Chabot Space Center.
  • The finish: a 10 mile descent into Berkeley, with only the occasional hill.

My first attempt

I told Harry about my ludicrous plan, and we agreed to leave on an early Friday morning to dodge the heat and traffic. We met at 5:00 AM and biked towards Palomares Rd. It's very surreal biking in the night and early morning; 84 was practically empty and the skies were beautiful. By the time we hit Palomares, I was already decently tired, with 40 more miles to go. Then the climb began, and I realized just how hard biking uphill actually was. I'd biked steeper hills before, but they ended quickly; this was a 5 mile slog of almost uninterrupted climbing. I was gassed within a mile, and had to walk my bike for a stretch before getting back on. Many, many breaks were taken. It was clear the full 50 miles wasn't happening today. After what felt like an eternity, we reached the final ascent and climbed up. After a quick break, we began the descent. In two minutes, it made all the pain worth it. It was probably 2 miles of continuous descent, faster than I'd ever gone on a bike. From there we coasted into Castro Valley and caught BART. I vowed to return.

Some preparation

It took more than a week to recover. I decided to attack the route one segment at a time.

Solo lap: Palomares

On a hot Friday at roughly 4 PM, I biked east to 84. The traffic shocked me more than the heat; I was constantly getting passed by cars, whereas maybe five had passed me and Harry the first time. But the heat was the real problem; I was drenched in sweat almost immediately. The only thing that helped was knowing the trail; I knew exactly how much of the climb was left. I stopped once before the summit, but reached the peak without walking, and the descent afterward felt fantastic. I was very encouraged: my legs were tired, but I could still function, unlike the first attempt, and this was in worse heat.

Solo lap: Redwood Rd

After a day of recovery, feeling undefeatable, I took BART to Castro Valley and started this segment at 8:00 AM. The first stretch was flat through suburbia, then an unexpectedly steep hill that was thankfully short. After a relaxing descent, the first real climb began. My legs were still tired from Palomares, but I made my way up, taking a break at the top. This stretch isn't shaded by redwoods like the rest of the road, which I did not enjoy in the heat. The descent that followed was gorgeous: redwoods and ferns everywhere. I'd hiked and driven through redwoods before, but biking through them was a different experience entirely. The second climb, out of Redwood Regional Park, was worse. There was more traffic, and by the halfway point in the suburbs I knew the hardest part was still ahead. It was an unrelenting grind; I dropped to my lowest gear and suffered to the top. Near the peak I passed a herd of goats clearing vegetation among the trees. It was an unusual sight, and I stopped to take it in. From there it was 10 calm miles down to Rockridge BART.

The second attempt

A week later, feeling invincible, I decided to combine both segments: Palomares, then as much of Redwood Rd as I could manage. I suspected that if I finished the first Redwood climb, I'd want the second too. It was a cool, cortisol-free Sunday morning when I started east. I cleared Palomares without a single break, unknowingly setting a PB. I stopped in Castro Valley to eat a small sandwich. Until I hit suburbia midway through the last climb, that was the only good part of the ride. The first Redwood climb went faster than before, and starting early meant I hit the redwoods before the midday heat. Then the second climb began, and I felt noticeably weaker: my legs couldn't sustain power the way they had before, and I was forced into several stops. I stopped again in the suburbs to eat some nuts. That turned out to be a mistake; I'd packed plenty of water but barely enough carbs, and I was about to pay for it. The second half of that climb was the worst pain I've ever felt biking. I willed myself up at what must have been walking pace, far worse than my first time up Palomares. By the final descent into Berkeley I had nothing left. Even the small rise near Claremont felt impossible. By the time I reached Panda Express near campus, I was dizzy, my hands were shaking, and I could barely order. Turns out I'd bonked: you need roughly 60g of carbs per hour of biking, and I'd forgotten to pack any snacks. I sat on a bench feeling like I could fall asleep, demolished a full plate, and rested for an hour before biking down to BART. I'd pushed myself to my limit, but I'd made it.

"People of the Philippines: I have returned."

Douglas MacArthur, after returning to the Philippines following the start of the American liberation during WWII , 1944

I thought I had accomplished a very difficult physical feat. 50 miles with 3800 feet of elevation gain is a lot, right? Nah. Some humbling perspective: a pro cyclist like Tadej Pogačar could probably do this entire route in Zone 2. It would barely register as a warmup. I try not to think about that too much. This whole experience has definitely made me like biking. It's something I hope to do more of once the semester starts. Hopefully one day, I'm in a position where I can complete this journey more easily.
