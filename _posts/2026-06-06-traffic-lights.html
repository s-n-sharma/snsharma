--- layout: post title: "A Rant on Traffic Lights and Public Transport" date: 2026-06-06 tags: [random] read_time: "2 min read" description: "it's 2026" mathjax: true --- A couple days ago, I was anxiously waiting to go forward at a traffic light, praying it would turn green quickly so I could catch the BART. Imagine my frustration when I saw that not a single car had turned left or crossed the intersection. And, as if to add insult to injury, it felt like the signal lasted forever. Now, it could just be that my perception of time was affected by my strong emotions, but it got me paying attention to traffic lights. Since then, I feel like I've noticed several instances where the light was clearly suboptimal.

It's 2026, why haven't we figured out traffic lights?

I know that some traffic lights have evolved to become very complex systems, and there's been a ton of research into this. But they could be better, and with all the advancements in computer science and machine learning, it feels like it's a solvable problem.

The East Bay-Peninsula gap

Adjacent to this is a big gripe I have with longer-distance public transport in the Bay Area like BART or Caltrain. From the East Bay, it feels like there's a dead zone at the Peninsula, specifically at San Mateo County and the surrounding areas. I have a dream that one day I'll be able to get on a BART train from the East Bay and reach any other city in the Bay in less than an hour. Seriously, the distance from the Hayward BART station to the San Mateo Caltrain station is less than the distance from Hayward to Berkeley, but it takes a 7 minute BART ride, a 1 hour bus ride, and then another 20 minute Caltrain ride, and that's not even factoring in the time spent waiting between each leg of the journey. It doesn't even have to be a train that connects the East Bay to the Peninsula; a dedicated bus lane would be enough! Some things I've been thinking about:
  • The Dumbarton Bridge has 6 lanes of traffic, 3 in each direction (if I remember correctly).
  • Do something like the zipper on the Golden Gate Bridge: 3 lanes of traffic for the congested direction, a dedicated bus lane for that direction, and 2 lanes for the less congested direction (buses going the less congested way don't need the dedicated lane).
  • Charge the big tech companies with private buses money for using that lane!
  • The San Mateo Bridge, at least the eastern span, is wide enough to fit some tracks too.
I hope that one day this dream of mine is realized.
